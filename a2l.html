A2L refrigerants &
the AIM Act

The R-410A era is ending. Residential A/C and heat-pump equipment built in the U.S. after January 1, 2025 ships with R-32 or R-454B — both classified A2L (mildly flammable) under ASHRAE Standard 34. This page is the field-ready summary of what changes for technicians: tools, spark sources, brazing, leak detection, cylinders.

Section 608 itself does not have a separate A2L cert. The exam covers A2L material inside Core and Type II. The bigger picture — the rules, the tools, the timeline — lives here.

A2L classification

ASHRAE Standard 34 · what the letters mean

ASHRAE Standard 34 assigns every refrigerant a two-character safety group. The first character is toxicity, the second is flammability:

  • A1Lower toxicity, no flame propagation. R-22, R-134a, R-410A.
  • A2LLower toxicity, mildly flammable — burning velocity ≤ 10 cm/s. R-32, R-454B, R-1234yf, R-454A, R-447A.
  • A2Lower toxicity, moderately flammable. R-152a.
  • A3Lower toxicity, highly flammable. R-290 (propane), R-600a (isobutane).
  • B1Higher toxicity, no flame propagation. R-123.

What "2L" actually means in the field

The "L" was added in 2010 specifically to separate refrigerants like R-32 — which technically burn but have very low burning velocity and high minimum ignition energy — from genuinely flammable refrigerants like propane.

  • Burning velocity≤ 10 cm/s — the flame moves slowly and self-extinguishes easily. R-32 is around 6.7 cm/s; propane is roughly 40 cm/s.
  • LFLLower Flammability Limit — minimum air concentration that will sustain a flame. R-32 ≈ 14 %, R-454B ≈ 11.3 %. A small leak in a normal room will not reach LFL.
  • Ignition energyA2Ls need a strong, sustained ignition source. A typical wall-switch arc or a static spark generally will not ignite them; a brazing torch absolutely will.
  • Heat of combustionA2Ls release less energy when they do burn than A2 or A3 refrigerants — the fire risk is meaningfully lower, but it is not zero.
Bottom line: Treat A2Ls with respect, not panic. The rules below exist to keep ignition sources away from the refrigerant during the few minutes when it could actually matter — mostly during service, when the system is open and a torch is in your hand.

The three refrigerants you'll meet

R-32 · R-454B · R-1234yf

R-32 — difluoromethane

  • GWP675 — about one-third of R-410A's 2088. ODP zero.
  • WhereResidential and light-commercial A/C, ductless mini-splits, heat pumps. Daikin chose it as their global R-410A replacement starting 2012; now widespread.
  • Pure or blendPure single-component refrigerant — no glide. Charges as either liquid or vapor without composition shift.
  • PressureOperating pressures very close to R-410A. Existing manifold gauges read sensibly; pressure-temperature charts differ slightly.
  • Recovery quirkNo glide means standard recovery procedures work, but the recovery machine still must be A2L-rated. Capacity and discharge temperatures run a bit higher than R-410A — check machine ratings.
  • OilPOE (polyol ester) — same family as R-410A. Keep containers capped; POE absorbs moisture aggressively.

R-454B — Opteon XL41

  • GWP466 — lowest GWP of the major R-410A replacements. ODP zero.
  • Composition68.9 % R-32 + 31.1 % R-1234yf blend. Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Rheem chose this as their R-410A replacement in U.S. residential.
  • GlideNear-azeotrope — temperature glide around 1.5 °F. Small enough to behave like a single refrigerant in practice, but charge as liquid only to keep the blend ratio.
  • Topping offAfter a leak, vapor leaks faster than liquid out of the blend — the remaining charge is slightly off-spec. EPA and most manufacturers allow topping-off for small losses; for large losses, recover and recharge.
  • PressureRoughly 5–7 % lower than R-410A at the same temperature. Uses the same SAE J639-style fittings as other A2Ls; not interchangeable with R-410A connections on new equipment.
  • OilPOE — same as R-410A. Existing R-410A oil-handling habits transfer cleanly.

R-1234yf — tetrafluoropropene (HFO)

  • GWP4 — basically negligible. The reason it exists.
  • WherePrimary use is motor vehicle A/C (regulated under Section 609, not 608). Required on new U.S. light vehicles since model year 2021. Also a building block in stationary blends like R-454B and R-513A.
  • Stationary useEmerging in chillers (R-1234ze and R-513A) and some commercial heat pumps. Pure R-1234yf in stationary equipment is rare but allowed under SNAP for select applications.
  • CostSignificantly more expensive per pound than R-32 or R-410A — the patent landscape and limited production capacity push the price up.
Quick take: If you service residential A/C, you will see R-32 and R-454B almost exclusively. R-1234yf shows up in cars (a 609 cert job) and in next-generation chillers. The blends are the future of stationary cooling.

The AIM Act phasedown

2022 baseline → 85 % cut by 2036

The American Innovation and Manufacturing (AIM) Act of 2020 directs EPA to phase down U.S. production and import of HFCs by 85 % from a 2011–2013 baseline over 15 years. The cuts are weighted by each refrigerant's GWP — pulling out high-GWP refrigerants like R-404A faster than lower-GWP ones like R-32.

  • 202210 % reduction — initial step-down. Production allowances issued on a GWP-weighted basis.
  • 202440 % reduction. Major production cut. Reclaim market starts to matter.
  • 2025EPA Technology Transitions rule effective: no new residential / light-commercial A/C or heat-pump equipment using R-410A. New units ship with R-32 or R-454B.
  • 2026Final EPA Refrigerant Management rule rolls in — leak repair threshold drops to 15 lb for many appliances, expanded reclaim and recordkeeping requirements.
  • 202970 % reduction. Virgin R-410A and R-404A production heavily constrained; service supply increasingly relies on reclaim.
  • 203480 % reduction.
  • 203685 % reduction — final step. The terminal phasedown level, set by statute.

What it means in service

  • Existing equipmentKeeps running. The AIM Act phases down new production, not legal use of installed refrigerant.
  • Reclaim becomes essentialService supply for R-410A, R-404A, R-134a will lean increasingly on reclaimed product. Recovering carefully — no venting, no cross-contamination — protects the supply you'll need next year.
  • Price signalHigh-GWP refrigerants get expensive fast as allowances tighten. Customers will increasingly weigh repair vs. replacement against refrigerant cost.

Leak detection on A2Ls

spark sources matter · UL & IEC ratings

Standard halogen-electronic leak detectors built for R-22 / R-410A may have internal arcing components that are fine in air but a hazard in a refrigerant-rich pocket near a leak. For A2L work you need a detector listed for class 2L refrigerants.

  • UL 60335-2-40U.S. equipment standard for A/C and heat pumps using A2Ls. Sets safety requirements for the equipment itself — sensors, refrigerant detection, lockouts.
  • IEC 60079International standard for equipment in explosive atmospheres. Ex-rated leak detectors built to 60079 are inherently safe in flammable-refrigerant environments.
  • Detector typesHeated diode and infrared (NDIR) detectors are commonly A2L-listed. Avoid corona-discharge or unshielded electronic detectors near a known leak.
  • SensitivityA2L-rated leak detectors typically resolve to 3–5 g/yr — sensitive enough for the EPA leak-rate verification tests.
  • Refrigerant-detection systemsUL 60335-2-40 equipment includes a built-in refrigerant detection sensor that triggers fan operation and shuts off heat strips on a leak. When servicing, do not bypass it permanently.

Pressure testing A2L systems

  • Use nitrogenSame as A1 work — pressure-test with dry nitrogen. Never compressed air or oxygen.
  • Trace gasA small trace of refrigerant for detector use is acceptable, but limit it — the goal is the smallest amount that lets the detector work.
  • No flame testHalide torches detect chlorine and are useless on chlorine-free A2Ls. Open flames near suspected leaks are obviously contraindicated.

Brazing & ventilation

recover first · ventilate · then heat

The single most important rule when working on A2L equipment: always recover the refrigerant before brazing. The risk is not the bulk system charge — it's a refrigerant pocket lingering near the joint when you strike the torch.

  • Recover fullyPull a vacuum to the standard target. Do not braze on a system holding any refrigerant pressure, A1 or A2L.
  • Nitrogen purgeFlow a small stream of dry nitrogen through the line while brazing. Prevents oxidation and displaces any residual refrigerant. 2–3 SCFH is plenty.
  • Ventilate the spaceIndoor work areas — closets, attics, basements — should have positive air movement. Open a window, run the air handler fan, or use a portable ventilator for confined spaces.
  • Spark sourcesTreat any spark or flame within ~3 ft of an open joint as a hazard. Includes drills, grinders, switching contactors, and yes — your phone if it slips out of your pocket.
  • Combustion appliancesPilot lights and water-heater burners count as ignition sources. If you're servicing equipment in the same room as a gas appliance with a standing pilot, shut the pilot off first.
  • Charge limitsUL 60335-2-40 sets maximum charge per room volume for A2Ls. Honor the equipment nameplate — you cannot legally upsize a charge past the listed limit.
Field practice: The discipline ESCO and contractor associations are pushing — recover, purge with N₂, ventilate, then braze — is already good practice on R-410A. A2L work just makes the consequences of skipping any step more visible.

Tools rated for A2L work

what's compatible · what isn't

Recovery machines

  • RequiredRecovery machine listed for class 2L refrigerants per UL 21010 (ARI 740 listing alone is not sufficient for A2Ls).
  • WhyA2L-rated machines isolate spark-producing components (motor brushes, switches, capacitors) from the refrigerant path, or use brushless / spark-free motors and sealed enclosures.
  • Backwards compatibilityMost A2L-listed machines also handle A1 refrigerants — buying A2L-rated covers your fleet.
  • Hose disciplineUse low-loss fittings (40 CFR 82.158). For A2Ls, also keep hoses short — minimizes residual vapor when you disconnect.

Vacuum pumps

  • Spark-free or ventedA2L-suitable pumps either use brushless motors or vent the motor housing away from the inlet. Older pumps with brushed motors and an internal switch should not be used to evacuate a system that may still contain A2L vapor.
  • Always evacuate firstBest practice: recover to spec, break vacuum with N₂, vent, then evacuate to 500 microns with the vacuum pump.

Manifold gauges & manometers

  • PressuresR-32 and R-454B run pressures close enough to R-410A that R-410A-rated manifolds work mechanically. Verify the rating stamped on the gauge.
  • Digital manometersA2L-rated digital gauges are intrinsically-safe rated and do not produce sparks at the sensor. Required for work in confined spaces with flammable charge.
  • HosesSAE J2196 hoses rated for high-pressure HFC service are suitable for R-32 / R-454B. Keep them in good condition — A2L compatibility tolerates no pinholes.

What does not need to change

  • Refrigerant scalesNo electrical exposure to refrigerant — fine.
  • Brazing rigsSame torch, same rod. The procedure changes — recover, purge, ventilate — but the equipment doesn't.
  • PPESame safety glasses and butyl-lined gloves. Liquid A2Ls cause frostbite the same as any other refrigerant.

Cylinder color codes

AHRI Guideline N · color tells you the refrigerant

Refrigerant cylinder colors are assigned by AHRI Guideline N, an industry standard. Colors are not legally enforced but are followed by every major U.S. supplier — they exist so a tech can identify a tank at a glance even if the label is gone.

  • R-32Pearl-effect off-white / cream with a red band at the shoulder marking A2L flammability.
  • R-454BLight green with a red band at the shoulder.
  • R-1234yfWhite with a red band. (Automotive cylinders are smaller, often packaged differently.)
  • R-410APink / rose. No red band — A1 refrigerant.
  • R-22Light green — same color as R-454B but without the red flammability band. Read the label.
  • R-134aLight blue.
  • Recovery cylinderGray body, yellow top (DOT 4BA / 4BW). Same recovery cylinder is used for A1 and A2L; mark it with the refrigerant inside.
The red band at the shoulder is the universal A2L flag — if the cylinder has one, the refrigerant is mildly flammable and the same precautions on this page apply.

Cylinder handling

  • 80 % ruleSame as A1 — never fill above 80 % of water capacity. Liquid expands with temperature; overfill ruptures the tank.
  • StorageCool, ventilated area. A2Ls prefer cooler temps for the same liquid-expansion reason; ventilation matters here in case of a slow leak.
  • DOT shippingA2Ls ship as "Refrigerant gas, n.o.s., 2.1" (Division 2.1 = flammable gas) — different from A1 refrigerants which ship as 2.2.

FAQ

quick answers · tech and tradesperson level

  • Do A2Ls need a separate cert?No. Section 608 covers all stationary refrigerants. EPA has not added an A2L-specific endorsement. Manufacturers and trade groups (ESCO, RSES) offer optional A2L training programs that some employers require.
  • Can I retrofit R-410A → R-454B?No. Retrofits between A1 and A2L equipment are not permitted. The equipment itself must be designed and listed for the A2L — UL 60335-2-40 changes the electrical and refrigerant-detection design.
  • Is R-32 patented?R-32 itself is not patented — it's a long-known molecule. Daikin held key patents on use in residential systems but offered royalty-free licenses, which accelerated adoption.
  • What about R-290 (propane)?Propane is A3 — significantly more flammable than A2L. SNAP has approved R-290 for some self-contained commercial refrigeration with charge limits around 150 g. It is not used in residential split A/C in the U.S.
  • Is CO₂ (R-744) coming?For supermarket refrigeration and some heat pumps — yes, increasingly. R-744 is A1 (non-flammable) and has GWP 1, but operates at much higher pressures (~1500 psi). Different cert risk profile from A2Ls.
  • When should I buy A2L tools?If you do residential A/C service, now. R-32 and R-454B units shipped through 2025 are out of warranty calls; field service hits this year. A2L-rated recovery + leak detector + manometer is the minimum kit.
